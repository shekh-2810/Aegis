Stay Safe Online

  • Check for HTTPS and a correctly spelled domain name before entering any details.
  • Never enter an OTP, password, or card PIN on a page you reached via an unsolicited link or message.
  • Use the "Check a link" scanner on the homepage before opening unfamiliar links.
  • Be suspicious of urgency — "act now or lose your account" is a classic pressure tactic.

Secure Your Accounts

  • Turn on two-factor authentication (2FA) on email, banking, and social accounts.
  • Use a unique password per account; a password manager makes this practical.
  • Review connected apps/devices periodically and remove ones you don't recognize.
  • Set up account-recovery details (backup email/phone) so you can regain access quickly if compromised.

Recognize Common Scams

  • Phishing: emails/SMS impersonating a bank or brand, linking to a lookalike login page.
  • UPI/payment scams: fake "collect" requests or refund calls that ask you to enter your UPI PIN.
  • Job/lottery scams: upfront "processing fees" for a job, prize, or loan that doesn't exist.
  • Typosquatting: domains that look almost identical to a real brand (e.g. an extra letter or different TLD).

Recover After an Incident

  1. Change the passwords of any account you think was compromised, starting with email.
  2. If money was lost, contact your bank/payment provider immediately — many have short windows to reverse fraud.
  3. File a report using the Report Incident flow to get a tracking code and a recovery guide.
  4. Keep evidence (screenshots, messages, transaction IDs) — you can attach these to your report.

Learn the Basics

  • Phishing: tricking someone into revealing credentials via a fake message or site.
  • Malware: software designed to damage, disrupt, or gain unauthorized access to a device.
  • Two-factor authentication (2FA): a second proof of identity beyond your password.
  • Risk score: how Aiges' scanner summarizes how many warning signs a link shows — higher means riskier.

Get Help

  • India: report cybercrime at cybercrime.gov.in or call the national helpline 1930.
  • Use Track Status to follow up on a report you already filed with Aiges.
  • Contact your bank's official fraud helpline (found on the back of your card or their official site) — never a number from a message.
